What to Know

Understanding the Options

Stump Grinding vs. Stump Removal

Both methods eliminate the stump from your property. The right approach depends on what you want to do with the space afterward.

Option One

Stump Grinding

Stump grinding uses specialized equipment to chip the stump down below the surface of the ground. The root system remains in place and naturally decays over time underground. Grinding is the more common approach for most residential situations — it removes the visible stump, allows the area to be covered with soil and mulch, and causes minimal disruption to the surrounding yard.

Best for: eliminating the stump without extensive excavation, and when you don't need the root system fully cleared.

Option Two

Stump Removal

Stump removal involves extracting both the stump and the major root structure from the ground. This is a more involved process that requires more excavation and leaves the area more fully cleared. It's often the better option when you need the ground completely free of root material — for example, when you're planning to plant a new tree in the same location or doing significant landscaping in the area.

Best for: situations where you need the root system fully out of the ground, not just the stump above grade.

Not sure which approach is right for your situation? Contact Barton's for a free assessment.

Common Situations

When Homeowners Deal With Stumps

Stump work isn't always the first thing homeowners think about, but these are the situations where it genuinely makes a difference.

Completing a Tree Removal

The tree is down, but the stump is still there. Addressing the stump finishes the job and restores full usability to the space. Handling both the removal and the stump at the same time is usually the most efficient approach.

Reclaiming Usable Yard Space

A stump in the middle of the lawn creates an ongoing mowing obstacle and can make landscaping or other yard projects harder to execute. Removing it gives you clean, usable space back.

Eliminating a Hazard

An exposed stump is a tripping hazard, particularly in yards where children or elderly family members spend time. Grinding the stump below grade eliminates the obstacle and reduces risk.

Dealing With an Old Stump

Older stumps attract insects and fungi as they decay. Some tree species will continue sending up root sprouts, constantly trying to regrow. Getting rid of the stump stops these ongoing issues.

Part of the Full Job

Stump Work & Tree Removal

Stump grinding and stump removal most naturally follow tree removal. When a tree comes down, the stump is what's left. Handling both at the same time is usually the most practical approach — the crew is already there, the site is already set up, and it avoids scheduling a separate visit.
